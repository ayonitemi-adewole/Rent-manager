# Switch gateways, cut prices, keep the margin

RentPadi's current plan has one flaw that matters more than all the others: it runs rent through Flutterwave, whose official Nigerian page now shows a flat, **uncapped 2% fee on local payments** ([Flutterwave NG pricing](https://flutterwave.com/ng/pricing)). On a ₦3M yearly rent that is **₦64,500 in gateway fees with VAT**, set against a Free-plan commission of ₦7,500. If the platform account absorbs that fee, as Flutterwave's split example shows ([Flutterwave split docs](https://developer.flutterwave.com/docs/collecting-payments/split-payments)), every large rent payment loses money. If the landlord or tenant absorbs it, the product becomes far more expensive than its rivals. The recommended revision moves rent collection to **Paystack**, which charges **1.5% + ₦100 capped at ₦2,000**, or **1% capped at ₦300** on dedicated virtual accounts ([Paystack pricing](https://paystack.com/pricing)). It makes the landlord's subaccount the fee bearer, so RentPadi's cut always arrives whole. It cuts the Free commission to **1% capped at ₦2,000 per rent bill** and re-prices the four tiers under the competition: **Padi Free** (≤5 units), **Padi** (₦2,400/month or ₦24,000/year, ≤10 units), **Padi Plus** (₦5,000 or ₦50,000, ≤25 units) and **Padi Pro** (₦10,000 or ₦100,000, ≤50 units, then ₦200 per extra unit). Tenants always pay exactly their rent, and nobody is charged VAT while RentPadi sits under the small-company threshold. At the planning rate of ₦1,500/$, fixed costs come to about **₦39,500 a month**, and every paid tier keeps a margin above **94%** for yearly-rent landlords. Break-even is about **21 Padi, 10 Padi Plus or 5 Padi Pro** landlords. A 50-landlord mix earns roughly **₦100k a month profit** on yearly billing (about ₦71k in a harsh stress case). That is less than the draft's ₦170k claim, but the draft's figure rested on gateway costs that no longer exist.

## The Flutterwave fee turns every large rent into a loss

The draft assumed Flutterwave charged "~1.4%, capped at ₦2,000" and flagged the doubt only in section 7. The research settles that doubt against the draft. Flutterwave's NG pricing page lists local transactions at **"2% per transaction"** ("1.4% transaction fee + 0.6% platform fee"), applies it to cards, bank, USSD and transfer alike, shows no cap, and adds **7.5% VAT** on fees ([Flutterwave NG pricing](https://flutterwave.com/ng/pricing)). The "capped at ₦2,000" figure appears only in third-party SEO blogs ([kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/paystack-vs-flutterwave-pricing-nigeria-2026)). Who bears the fee is also unclear. The pricing page says customers bear it by default. The split-payment docs work an example in which the fee comes out of the main account's share ("6000 − 4200 − 60 = 1740") and add a **₦50 stamp duty** debited from settlement on payments above ₦10,000 ([Flutterwave split docs](https://developer.flutterwave.com/docs/collecting-payments/split-payments)).

The arithmetic below compares one rent payment under each route. Every gateway figure includes 7.5% VAT. The research could not confirm whether Paystack's Nigerian fees carry VAT, so the table assumes they do, which is the cautious choice.

| Rent payment | Flutterwave 2% × 1.075 | Paystack card: min(1.5% + ₦100, ₦2,000) × 1.075 | Paystack DVA: min(1%, ₦300) × 1.075 | Draft Free fee: min(1.5%, ₦7,500) | New Free fee: min(1%, ₦2,000) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₦100,000 | ₦2,150 | ₦1,720 | ₦322.50 | ₦1,500 | ₦1,000 |
| ₦500,000 | ₦10,750 | ₦2,150 | ₦322.50 | ₦7,500 | ₦2,000 |
| ₦1,000,000 | ₦21,500 | ₦2,150 | ₦322.50 | ₦7,500 | ₦2,000 |
| ₦3,000,000 | ₦64,500 | ₦2,150 | ₦322.50 | ₦7,500 | ₦2,000 |

Read across the bottom row. If the main account bears Flutterwave's fee, the draft loses **₦7,500 − ₦64,500 = −₦57,000** on one Free-plan yearly rent of ₦3M. On subscriber plans, where RentPadi takes 0%, it would lose the whole **₦64,500**. Even a ₦100k monthly rent loses ₦1,500 − ₦2,150 = −₦650 per payment. If the fee is instead pushed onto the landlord, a Free landlord pays ₦7,500 + ₦64,500 = **₦72,000 (2.4%)** to collect ₦3M. Under the revised plan the same landlord pays ₦2,000 + ₦322.50 = **₦2,322.50 (0.08%)**. Flutterwave's 2% beats Paystack only on small amounts between ₦2,500 and about ₦20,000, where Paystack's ₦100 dominates, and the gap there is ₦25–₦90 ([Paystack pricing](https://paystack.com/pricing)). Rent payments are large, so switching is the clearest single gain available. Paystack also lets a split name its fee bearer: by default the main account bears it, but passing `bearer: "subaccount"` makes the landlord's share carry it, and the request fails if that share cannot cover the fee ([Paystack split docs](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/split-payments/)). That is exactly the "take a clean cut, never hold funds" model RentPadi wants.

Two caveats keep this from being automatic. First, the research did **not** confirm that Paystack dedicated virtual accounts can be split to a landlord's subaccount. If they cannot, transfer money would land in RentPadi's own balance. That breaks the never-hold-rent rule, and the licensing analysis shows why the rule matters: a PSSP "cannot hold customer funds", and pooled custody points toward an MMO licence with ₦5bn+ capital ([Mondaq, Aug 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/navigating-nigerian-fintech-regulations-cbn-sec-and-ndic-licensing-guide/1828710)). In that case, use Paystack Checkout's transfer channel at the standard capped rate, which is still at most ₦2,150. Second, Flutterwave may quote a capped or negotiated rate in writing, and a June 2026 CBN concentration circular may restructure payment groups in 2027 ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/06/16/nigerias-central-bank-orders-fintechs-to-split-their-payments-empires-or-divest/)). So the code should keep a gateway layer that can switch back, rather than delete Flutterwave.

## Four Padi tiers priced just under every rival

The draft's paid tiers sit above the visible market. Ledge charges **₦0 for 5 units, ₦5k/month for 15 and ₦10k/month for 50**, and passes on only a 1.5% fee capped at ₦2,000 ([Ledge](https://ledgeapp.co/)). LandlordNG sells **₦60,000/year for 30 units** and advertises "0% rent commission" ([LandlordNG](https://landlordng.com/)). RentFlow's Agent plan is **₦20,000/year for 10 properties** ([RentFlow](https://rentflow.ng/)). Property360's Founding Pro is **₦65,000/year**, against a regular price of ₦81,600 ([Property360](https://www.property360.africa/)). Against these, the draft's Standard (₦6,000 for 15 units) costs more than Ledge Starter for the same units. Its Pro (₦15,000 for 40) costs more than Ledge Growth (₦10,000 for 50). Its ₦7,500 Free cap is 3.75× the ₦2,000 reference point landlords will see elsewhere. Ledge is also running a promotion that gives everything free until 2 February 2027 ([Ledge](https://ledgeapp.co/)), so RentPadi will launch into a price-sensitive moment.

The revised tiers keep the founder's four-plan limit and adopt the Padi names throughout (`padi_free | padi | padi_plus | padi_pro`).

| | **Padi Free** | **Padi** | **Padi Plus** | **Padi Pro** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | ₦0 | ₦2,400 | ₦5,000 | ₦10,000 |
| Yearly (2 months free) | ₦0 | ₦24,000 | ₦50,000 | ₦100,000 |
| Units | up to 5 | up to 10 | up to 25 | up to 50, then ₦200/unit/month |
| RentPadi fee on online rent | 1%, max ₦2,000 per rent bill | none | none | none |
| Gateway fee on rent | landlord, at cost | landlord, at cost | landlord, at cost | landlord, at cost |
| Reminders | 2 SMS per rent bill | WhatsApp + SMS fallback | WhatsApp + SMS fallback | WhatsApp + SMS fallback |
| Caretakers | — | — | 1 | up to 5 |

Each choice has a reason. Padi's monthly price of **₦2,400** sits under Paystack's ₦2,500 threshold, so the ₦100 fixed fee is waived: the gateway cost is 1.5% × ₦2,400 = ₦36, or ₦38.70 with VAT, instead of ₦137.50 ([Paystack pricing](https://paystack.com/pricing)). Ten units for ₦24,000 a year matches RentFlow's ₦20,000 for 10 and adds WhatsApp. Padi Plus gives **25 units for ₦5,000**, against Ledge's 15 units for the same price. Padi Pro matches Ledge Growth's **₦10,000 for 50 units** and adds five caretaker logins, which Ledge only offers on its ₦20k Agency tier. The yearly discount (2 months free, about 17%) matches RentFlow and sits between OurProperty's ≈8% and Bumpa's "up to 20%" ([RentFlow](https://rentflow.ng/), [Bumpa](https://getbumpa.com/pricing)).

Padi Free shrinks from 10 to 5 units because the market's free tiers run 1–5 units: LandlordNG gives 3 and Ledge gives 5 ([LandlordNG](https://landlordng.com/), [Ledge](https://ledgeapp.co/)). It also shrinks because the lower cap removes the draft's reason for a big Free tier, which was that "Free earns on every unit". At ₦2,000 per yearly bill, a Free unit earns less than the ₦2,400 a year each unit brings on a full Padi plan (₦24,000 ÷ 10). The cap now applies **per rent bill, not per payment**. The draft's per-payment cap would have charged a tenant who pays a ₦1M rent in four instalments 4 × ₦2,000 = ₦8,000 instead of ₦2,000. That is unfair, and it pushes tenants off-platform. Raising prices would be the alternative. The market evidence argues against it: margins are already above 94% (next section), so landlord count matters more than price, and the research found no measured willingness-to-pay data for Nigeria, so the pilot interview question should decide any increase.

## Margins stay above 94% even after Meta's October change

Two cost inputs changed since the draft. From **1 October 2026**, utility templates sent inside the 24-hour window are charged. Service replies are free only for the **first 1,000 per number per month**, then cost about $0.0067 each ([Meta pricing docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). The Nigerian utility rate itself is unchanged at **$0.0067** ([ChatMaxima](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/nigeria/), [Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/chatapp/whatsapp-message-rate-adjustments-and-service-conversation-message-billing)). The official naira closed at **₦1,357.19/$** on 16 November 2026, with the parallel market up to ₦1,445 ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/dollar-to-naira-exchange-rate-today-november-17-2026)). This report plans at **₦1,500/$**, about 10% weaker than official, to cover bank-card FX spreads. That gives a WhatsApp message cost of $0.0067 × 1,500 × 1.075 VAT = **₦10.80, rounded to ₦11**. SMS costs ₦6.49 + 7.5% VAT = **₦6.98, rounded to ₦7** ([BulkSMSNigeria](https://www.bulksmsnigeria.com/pricing)), which is above the draft's ₦5.

For each tenant and each rent bill, paid plans send 3 reminders, 1 receipt and 1 landlord alert. That is 5 × ₦11 = **₦55**. If the bot's chat replies (about 5 per rent bill) go past the free 1,000, add 5 × ₦11 = ₦55, for **₦110** in the stress case. Padi Free sends 2 SMS reminders, 1 WhatsApp receipt and 1 landlord alert: 2 × ₦7 + 2 × ₦11 = **₦36**. Fixed costs are Supabase Pro at $25 × 1,500 = **₦37,500** ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)), Cloudflare Pages at **₦0** ([Cloudflare limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)), and about **₦2,000/month** for domains (.com.ng ≈ ₦3,000/yr plus .com ≈ ₦15–20k/yr) ([BrandSpur](https://brandspurng.com/2026/03/25/whogohost-launches-nigerias-cheapest-com-ng-and-ng-domains-at-%E2%82%A63000-and-%E2%82%A69200-for-entrepreneurs)). That totals **₦39,500**. Using the direct Cloud API rather than a BSP avoids Termii's $50/month per WhatsApp account and 360dialog's €49 per number ([Termii](https://termii.com/pricing), [360dialog](https://360dialog.com/pricing)).

The table uses typical portfolios of 7 units on Padi, 15 on Padi Plus and 35 on Padi Pro, all billed yearly. RentPadi's own gateway cost on the subscription is card rate × 1.075, spread over 12 months: Padi (₦24,000 × 1.5% + ₦100) × 1.075 = ₦494.50/yr, or ₦41.20/month. Padi Plus is ₦913.75/yr (₦76.10/month). Padi Pro is ₦1,720/yr (₦143.30/month).

| Case (yearly billing) | Revenue/month | Gateway | Messages | Contribution | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Padi, 7 units, yearly rent | ₦2,000 | ₦41 | 7 × ₦55 ÷ 12 = ₦32 | ₦1,927 | 96% |
| Padi, 7 units, monthly rent, chat charged | ₦2,000 | ₦41 | 7 × ₦110 = ₦770 | ₦1,189 | 59% |
| Padi Plus, 15 units, yearly rent | ₦4,167 | ₦76 | 15 × ₦55 ÷ 12 = ₦69 | ₦4,022 | 97% |
| Padi Plus, 15 units, monthly rent, chat charged | ₦4,167 | ₦76 | 15 × ₦110 = ₦1,650 | ₦2,441 | 59% |
| Padi Pro, 35 units, yearly rent | ₦8,333 | ₦143 | 35 × ₦55 ÷ 12 = ₦160 | ₦8,030 | 96% |
| Padi Pro, 35 units, monthly rent, chat charged | ₦8,333 | ₦143 | 35 × ₦110 = ₦3,850 | ₦4,340 | 52% |
| Padi Free, 4 units, yearly rent, 60% online | 4 × 0.6 × ₦2,000 ÷ 12 = ₦400 | ₦0 (landlord bears) | 4 × (₦14 + 0.6 × ₦22) ÷ 12 = ₦9 | ₦391 | 98% |

Even the worst corner stays positive. A Padi Pro landlord at the full 50 units, on monthly rent, with every chat charged, costs 50 × ₦110 = ₦5,500 against ₦8,333 − ₦143, a ₦2,690 contribution. Extra Pro units at ₦200/month each cost at most ₦110. The only loss-making customer is a Padi Free landlord whose tenants never pay online: 5 tenants × ₦14 of SMS = ₦70 per rent bill, with no income. That is an acceptable acquisition cost.

**Break-even** is fixed cost ÷ contribution: ₦39,500 ÷ ₦1,927 = 20.5, so **21 Padi landlords**. ₦39,500 ÷ ₦4,022 = 9.8, so **10 Padi Plus landlords**. ₦39,500 ÷ ₦8,030 = 4.9, so **5 Padi Pro landlords**. Padi Free alone would need about ₦39,500 ÷ ₦391 ≈ **101 landlords**. The draft's assumption that a Free landlord earns "₦3k a month" no longer holds: at a ₦2,000 yearly cap that would need 18 online yearly payments a year. Free is a funnel, not a profit centre.

**The 50-landlord example** uses 15 Padi Free (4 units), 15 Padi (7), 14 Padi Plus (15) and 6 Padi Pro (35), which is 585 units. On yearly billing and yearly rent:

| Plan | Landlords | Revenue/month | Gateway | Messages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Padi Free | 15 | 15 × ₦400 = ₦6,000 | ₦0 | 15 × ₦9 = ₦136 |
| Padi | 15 | 15 × ₦2,000 = ₦30,000 | ₦618 | ₦481 |
| Padi Plus | 14 | 14 × ₦4,167 = ₦58,333 | ₦1,066 | ₦963 |
| Padi Pro | 6 | 6 × ₦8,333 = ₦50,000 | ₦860 | ₦963 |
| **Total** | **50** | **₦144,333** | **₦2,544** | **₦2,542** |

Profit is ₦144,333 − ₦2,544 − ₦2,542 − ₦39,500 = **₦99,747 a month**. If every subscriber paid monthly instead, revenue would be ₦172,000 and profit **₦125,131**. In a stress case where every tenant pays monthly rent and every chat reply is charged, messages rise to ₦61,362 and profit falls to **₦70,927**. At ₦1,600/$, fixed costs rise to ₦42,000 and WhatsApp to ₦11.50, which trims about ₦2,700 from the base case. The chat assumption in the base case holds: 585 yearly-rent units produce about 49 rent bills a month × 5 replies ≈ 244 replies, well under Meta's free 1,000.

One-off costs fit comfortably inside ₦500k. A private company at CAC costs ₦10,000 for ≤₦1m share capital, plus ₦8,500 stamp duty, ₦1,000–₦5,000 name reservation and ₦30,000–₦65,000 in agent fees ([Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/ask-legit/guides/1709408-everything-budget-cac-registration-fees-nigeria/)), about ₦50k–₦89k in total. Add six months of fixed costs (6 × ₦39,500 = ₦237,000) and a ₦20,000 SMS float. The total is roughly **₦307k–₦346k**.

## Fairness holds for landlords, tenants and the taxman

Tenants pay **exactly the rent** on every plan. RentPadi never adds a platform fee or VAT to the tenant's bill, and paying by bank transfer is never surcharged. The legal ₦50 stamp duty on transfers of ₦10,000 or more falls on the sender by law from 1 January 2026 ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/12/31/bank-transfers-senders-to-pay-n50-stamp-duty-from-january-1-2026)). That is a national charge, not a RentPadi fee, and receipts should say so. Global tools fund free tiers with flat tenant fees: TurboTenant charges tenants $2 per ACH payment and Avail $2.50 ([Hemlane](https://hemlane.com/resources/free-landlord-software-who-pays/)). The founder's reason for rejecting that model still holds: a surprise charge at checkout pushes tenants toward unrecorded transfers. The plan keeps one door open. Landlords may later choose to pass the **card** gateway fee to tenants who pick card over transfer, shown before payment. Paystack offers such a toggle, and the research found no Nigerian rule against it ([Paystack support](https://support.paystack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009973579-Can-I-pass-the-transaction-charges-to-my-customers-)).

For landlords, the cost stays small next to their rent roll. Each line below adds the subscription to the gateway fee on DVA transfers (₦322.50 per payment). A Padi landlord with 7 units at ₦500k pays ₦24,000 + 7 × ₦322.50 = ₦26,258 on ₦3.5m, which is **0.75%**. A Padi Plus landlord with 15 units at ₦1m pays ₦50,000 + ₦4,838 = ₦54,838 on ₦15m, **0.37%**. A Padi Pro landlord with 35 units at ₦1.5m pays ₦100,000 + ₦11,288 = ₦111,288 on ₦52.5m, **0.21%**. A Padi Free landlord with 4 tenants at ₦400k paying online pays 4 × ₦2,322.50 = ₦9,290 on ₦1.6m, **0.58%**. Monthly-rent landlords on Free pay more: with 4 tenants at ₦100k, 4 × (₦1,000 + ₦322.50) = ₦5,290 a month on ₦400k, **1.3%**. Padi would cut that to ₦2,400 + ₦1,290 = ₦3,690, or 0.9%. That is why the "you'd save" card should appear from the third monthly tenant: 3 × ₦1,000 = ₦3,000 already exceeds ₦2,400. For context, Lagos law allows agents to take up to 10% agency plus 10% legal fees on a letting, and reported Ikorodu cases reach 37.5% each ([Nairametrics, Oct 2025](https://nairametrics.com/2025/10/17/lagos-tenants-pay-double-rent-cost-as-agents-defy-10-rule/)). Rents themselves are quoted yearly: the Ibadan median listing is about ₦1.6m and Ikorodu's about ₦996k ([NPC Oyo](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/market-trends/average-prices/for-rent/flats-apartments/oyo), [NPC Lagos](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/market-trends/average-prices/for-rent/flats-apartments/lagos)). So most landlords will hit the ₦2,000 cap and pay well under 0.5%.

The draft's VAT line was wrong for today. VAT stays at **7.5%**. Businesses with turnover up to the threshold and ≤₦250m fixed assets are exempt from VAT registration, charging and remittance ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/ng/en/services/tax/nigeria-s-tax-reform-in-focus/2025-nta-vat-key-business-changes)). The threshold itself is disputed: NTA s.202 says **₦50m**, NTAA s.147 says **₦100m**, and the reform chair calls ₦50m "an error" ([TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/spotted-nigeria-tax-act-contradicts-ntaa-on-turnover-cap-for-small-business)). At ₦144k a month (about ₦1.7m a year), RentPadi is under either figure. It should charge **no VAT**, print "VAT not charged: small business" on invoices, and review at ₦40m using ₦50m as the safe trigger. Rent is VAT-exempt ([ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/02/16/nigeria-tax-act-2025-setting-the-record-straight-on-housing-vat-and-the-25-tax-claim)). Contracts should frame RentPadi as a collection-software provider, so that only its own fees and subscriptions count as turnover. A limited company also gets **0% CIT** below the threshold, which a business name does not ([EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1388-nigeria-tax-act-2025-has-been-signed-highlights)).

## Risks and checks before launch

The largest open risk is operational, not commercial. Several things should be confirmed in sandbox or in writing before any landlord pays. Can Paystack DVAs carry a split to a landlord subaccount with the landlord as bearer? Does Paystack add VAT to Nigerian fees? Does Paystack Checkout's pay-with-transfer channel bill at 1.5% + ₦100 or at the ₦300-capped rate? Do refunds reverse the RentPadi split? Will Flutterwave confirm in writing that no cap applies ([Flutterwave NG pricing](https://flutterwave.com/ng/pricing))? Meta's Nigeria figures come from mirrors of the rate card, and one Nigerian outlet quotes $0.0101 instead ([Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/technology/1728475-which-whatsapp-users-pay-n14-message-what-metas-billing-means-nigerians/)). Even at that rate a message costs about ₦16, and the yearly-rent margins above barely move. Monthly-rent stress margins would fall to about 30–40%: for Padi Pro with 35 units, 35 × 10 × ₦16.30 = ₦5,705, against ₦8,190 left after the gateway fee, a 30% margin. The ₦50m/₦100m conflict needs an accountant's view, and the founder needs a lawyer's view that a split-only platform needs no licence of its own: the research found no explicit CBN statement exempting it ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/navigating-nigerian-fintech-regulations-cbn-sec-and-ndic-licensing-guide/1828710)). NYSC bye-laws tell corps members "do not engage in private practice", with at least a 21-day unpaid extension as the penalty ([Awajis](https://awajis.com/nysc-bye-laws-during-the-period-of-primary-assignment/)). The founder should keep the business clear of primary-assignment hours and check before registering as a director. On the commercial side, willingness-to-pay, the share of tenants who pay online and recurring-card failure rates are all unmeasured. The pilot interview, not this model, should set the final numbers.

## Conclusion

The draft was not losing money on pricing. It was losing money on plumbing. Once the gateway fee is capped and assigned to the landlord's subaccount, RentPadi's costs per landlord become so small (₦32–₦160 a month for yearly-rent portfolios) that price is mainly a growth lever, not a cost-recovery one. That changes the strategy. RentPadi can undercut Ledge and LandlordNG at every size and still clear 94% contribution margins. It can tell landlords plainly that "tenants pay exactly the rent". And it can reach break-even with about ten mid-sized landlords. The real constraint on profit is the share of rent that flows online. Every manual transfer is revenue lost on Padi Free and engagement lost on paid plans, so product effort should go into making the virtual-account transfer the easiest way for a tenant to pay.
